## 5W1H Orientation

### What

An Identity Attribute is an information-bearing characteristic used to describe, distinguish, or help resolve an identity within a declared scope.

### Why

Names, dates, physical characteristics, roles, affiliations, appearance descriptors, and other attributes often participate in identity resolution but do not by themselves constitute identity.

### Who

Applicable to people, animals, plants, objects, devices, organizations, digital entities, and other referents.

### Where

Physical records, digital records, images, databases, metadata, forms, identity systems, and observational descriptions.

### When

An attribute is interpreted at a particular time or interval and may change without changing the underlying identity.

### How

An attribute may be observed, measured, declared, inherited from a record, or derived from another representation. Its evidential status should be preserved.

## Semantic Definition

**Identity Attribute** is a characteristic or descriptive value associated with an identity and used to describe or distinguish that identity within a declared context.

## Scope / Boundary

An attribute is not automatically an identifier and is not automatically stable. A property owned by the Property layer may become an identity-relevant attribute in a specific identity context; the semantic ownership of the underlying characteristic remains with the Property layer.

## Core Distinctions

**Attribute vs Identifier:** attributes describe; identifiers denote/reference.

**Observed vs Declared Attribute:** observation and declaration have different evidence provenance.

**Stable vs Mutable Attribute:** some attributes are relatively persistent; others are expected to change.

**Identity-Relevant vs Merely Descriptive:** relevance depends on the declared identity task and scope.

## Cross-Domain Significance

Identity attributes may originate from Properties, Quantities, States, Representations, Biometrics, Relations, or provenance records.

## Trust / Evidence

Every identity attribute should retain source, observation time where relevant, representation context, and uncertainty when those factors affect interpretation.

## Visitor Universe

Novice: an attribute is a description, not the identity.

Expert: attribute provenance, temporal validity, uncertainty, normalization, and cross-source conflict.
