## 5W1H Orientation

### What
Perception is a reusable semantic concept denoting an observer-relative perceptual phenomenon, experience, organization, appearance, or perceptual attribute associated with sensory or other input information.

### Why
Physical stimuli, observations, measurements, representations, and observer models do not by themselves constitute the perceptual phenomenon associated with an observer. A distinct Perception layer prevents physical, computational, evidential, and perceptual semantics from being collapsed.

### Who
Perception concerns an observer or observing population. The observer may be human or another explicitly modeled observing entity. Scientific study may involve psychophysicists, vision scientists, colour scientists, imaging researchers, engineers, clinicians, display researchers, and AI researchers, but visitor expertise does not determine semantic identity.

### Where
Perceptual phenomena may occur across visual, colour, spatial, temporal, depth, motion, material, image-quality, display, scene, multisensory, and other observation domains.

### When
Perception is associated with a temporal locus or interval and may depend on observer characteristics, stimulus, adaptation, task, context, condition, viewing geometry, illumination, surround, expectation, prior experience, or other qualifying circumstances.

### How
Perception may be investigated through psychophysical procedures, observer responses, reports, behavioural measures, physiological evidence, computational models, or other representations. These methods, responses, models, and representations are not themselves the Perception concept.

## Semantic Definition

**Perception** is a reusable canonical semantic concept denoting an observer-relative perceptual phenomenon, experience, organization, appearance, or perceptual attribute arising in relation to sensory or other input information under relevant qualifying conditions.

The generic concept does not require a particular biological mechanism, implementation, numerical measurement, conscious verbal report, or specific representation.

## Ontological Status

Perception is a semantic phenomenon concept. It is neither the observing entity nor the observing act, physical characteristic, measurable quantity, value, contextual frame, state, representation, process, activity, model, nor metric used to investigate or describe it.

A perceptual assertion may be qualified by observer, stimulus, task, context, condition, spatial and temporal parameters, adaptation, or other relevant factors without making those qualifiers part of the identity of generic Perception.

## Core Distinctions

`Perception ≠ Observer` — Observer identifies the observing entity or participant.

`Perception ≠ Observer Model` — Observer Model specifies or predicts observer response behaviour; Perception denotes the perceptual phenomenon being described.

`Perception ≠ Sensation` — Sensation and perception are distinct semantic responsibilities even though scientific and physiological models may connect them in different ways. GIOP does not require a universal serial sensation-to-perception pipeline.

`Perception ≠ Observation` — Observation is an observing act or activity; a perceptual phenomenon may be investigated in connection with an observation but is not the observing act.

`Perception ≠ Property` — Property denotes an attributable characteristic; a perceptual attribute or appearance is not automatically a physical property of its stimulus.

`Perception ≠ Quantity` — a perceptual phenomenon may be quantified, but the phenomenon is not automatically the quantitative concept used to characterize it.

`Perception ≠ Quantity Value` — a particular magnitude, rating, score, or reported value may provide evidence about perception without becoming the perception itself.

`Perception ≠ Condition / Context` — conditions and contexts qualify perceptual assertions; they do not replace the perceptual phenomenon.

`Perception ≠ State` — a recognized temporal mode of a bearer is distinct from a perceptual phenomenon associated with an observer.

`Perception ≠ Representation` — image, report, dataset, label, equation, feature vector, or software object may represent perceptual information without becoming the perception.

`Perception ≠ Model` — a mathematical, statistical, computational, ideal, empirical, or hybrid model may explain or predict perceptual behaviour without being the perceptual phenomenon.

`Perception ≠ Metric` — a psychophysical or quality metric evaluates or characterizes evidence; it is not automatically the perceptual phenomenon evaluated.

`Perception ≠ Cognition` — perception and cognition may interact closely, but recognition, categorization, understanding, reasoning, decision, and action require independent responsibility analysis.

## Perceptual Phenomenon Scope

The Perception layer currently covers the following content families:

- perceptual experience;
- perceptual organization;
- perceptual appearance;
- perceptual attributes;
- spatial and temporal perceptual phenomena;
- perceptual constancy and transformation;
- perceptual illusion and related non-veridical phenomena;
- specialized perceptual domains.

These families provide semantic coverage and retrieval structure. They do not constitute an automatic universal inheritance hierarchy.

## Perceptual Experience

Perceptual experience concerns the experienced aspect of a perceptual phenomenon. Conscious accessibility or verbal report may qualify a claim, but consciousness and reportability are not universal identity criteria for Perception.

A claim about perceptual experience must preserve its observer, stimulus, task, condition, and evidence scope where those factors materially affect interpretation.

## Perceptual Organization

Perception includes organization of input into perceptually coherent structures. Relevant research phenomena include grouping, figure-ground organization, contour integration, completion, segmentation, and related structural organization.

These are retained as content families unless a particular phenomenon demonstrates an independently reusable semantic responsibility that justifies canonical promotion.

## Perceptual Appearance

Perceptual appearance concerns how an object, surface, image, stimulus, or other entity is perceptually experienced with respect to attributes such as colour, size, shape, texture, gloss, transparency, or opacity.

`Visual Appearance` and `Color Appearance` are controlled specialization candidates. They remain distinct from the physical Property, Quantity, stimulus, and Representation layers.

## Perceptual Attributes

Perceptual attributes describe characteristics of perceptual experience or appearance. Examples supported by established colour terminology include hue, brightness, lightness, colourfulness, and chroma.

A perceptual attribute may be related to a physical quantity without being identical to it.

Example:

`Luminance → Quantity`  
`Brightness → Perceptual Attribute`  
`Brightness Rating → Response / Value / Result semantics`

Likewise, perceived contrast must not be silently equated with a physical contrast quantity or a contrast-sensitivity metric.

## Spatiotemporal Perception

Spatial, temporal, depth, and motion perception are recognized perceptual content families.

They may be qualified by spatial field, viewing geometry, temporal regime, stimulus dynamics, and task. Physical spatial or temporal quantities remain owned by the appropriate Quantity layer.

`Distance ≠ Perceived Distance`  
`Temporal Quantity ≠ Temporal Perception`  
`Motion Measurement ≠ Motion Perception`

## Perceptual Constancy and Transformation

Perceptual constancy concerns relative stability of perceptual experience or appearance despite relevant changes in stimulus or conditions. Colour, size, and shape constancy are representative research families.

Perceptual illusion and related non-veridical phenomena remain within Perception when their primary responsibility is the perceptual phenomenon itself. They are not automatically measurement errors, false data, or invalid representations.

## Observer / Stimulus / Task Qualification

A perceptual assertion may be qualified by:

- observer or observer population;
- stimulus or input;
- task or purpose;
- spatial field and viewing geometry;
- temporal regime;
- adaptation;
- illumination and surround;
- capture or display conditions;
- expectation or prior experience;
- relevant Context and Condition;
- reporting or measurement method.

These are qualifiers or relations unless an independent semantic responsibility is established.

## Psychophysical Establishment

Psychophysics investigates perceptual phenomena by controlling stimuli and tasks and recording observer responses. Thresholds, ratings, response times, discrimination results, sensitivity measures, and derived metrics are evidence or characterization outputs.

The evidence pathway may be represented conceptually as:

`Stimulus → Procedure / Task → Observer → Response / Evidence → Measurement / Analysis → Scoped Perceptual Claim`

This is an establishment pattern, not a definition of Perception.

`Psychophysical Response ≠ Perception`  
`Measurement Result ≠ Perception`  
`Metric ≠ Perception`

## Evidence and Validation

Perceptual claims require evidence appropriate to their intended scope. Relevant evidence may include controlled psychophysical experiments, standardized observer functions, repeated observer measurements, population studies, physiological evidence, validated computational models, and convergent scientific literature.

A computational prediction is not automatically a perceptual fact. A subjective report is evidence about an observer response but is not a universal law without appropriate scope and evidence.

`OBSERVED ≠ UNIVERSALLY TRUE`  
`MODELED ≠ EXPERIENCED`  
`REPORTED ≠ UNIVERSALLY VALID`

Material claims should preserve source, evidence type, scope, review status, version, and unresolved questions where applicable.

## Cross-Domain Significance

Perception provides a semantic bridge between physical or informational inputs and observer-relative phenomena without collapsing either side into the other. It supports imaging, colour science, display evaluation, human-computer interaction, visual quality assessment, material appearance, psychophysics, and multimodal observation while remaining independent of any one implementation.

## Semantic Relations

Potential relations include `experienced-by`, `elicited-by`, `associated-with`, `qualified-by`, `modeled-by`, `reported-by`, `measured-by`, and `represented-by`. Relation identity and formal semantics are governed by the Relations layer and are not redefined here.

## Machine and AI Interpretation

A machine should distinguish the perceptual phenomenon from its observer, stimulus, observer model, measurement, response value, and representation. Retrieval should use `SEM-PERCEPTION-GENERIC-001` where generic Perception is intended and should preserve material qualifiers such as observer population, task, stimulus, context, and conditions.

AI systems must not infer that a perceptual label is an objective physical property merely because the label is attached to an image, signal, dataset, or model output.

## Trust

The generic Perception concept is supported by convergent terminology and research. Specialized perceptual claims require scoped evidence and should not be promoted solely from frequency of use or intuitive plausibility.

GIOP canonical status is distinct from external scientific certainty: external evidence informs the canonical decision, while the registry preserves provenance and candidate disposition.

## Lifecycle

**Current State:** Active

Canonical lifecycle vocabulary:

`DRAFT → REVIEW → VALIDATED → ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED → ARCHIVED`

Changes in evidence, observer population, task, model, or representation may qualify or version an assertion without necessarily creating a new generic Perception concept. A new canonical concept requires an independently reusable semantic responsibility.

## Canonicalization Boundary

A term belongs in the Perception layer when its primary responsibility is a perceptual phenomenon, experience, organization, appearance, or perceptual attribute.

Route the term elsewhere when its primary responsibility is:

- stimulus or physical input;
- sensory response / sensation;
- observing entity;
- observing activity;
- physical Property;
- Quantity or Quantity Value;
- Condition or Context;
- State;
- Representation or Dataset;
- Process or Activity;
- Model or Algorithm;
- Metric, rating, or result;
- recognition, understanding, decision, or another independently governed cognitive responsibility.
