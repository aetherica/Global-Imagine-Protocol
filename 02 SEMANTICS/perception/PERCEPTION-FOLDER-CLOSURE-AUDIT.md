# GIOP V3.1 — Perception Folder Closure Audit

**Artifact Type:** Semantic Layer Closure Audit  
**Semantic Layer:** Perception  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Status:** ACTIVE / CLOSED FOR V3.1 GENERIC CORE SCOPE  
**Authority:** GIOP  
**Branch:** `v3.1-tree-architecture`

## Closure Decision

The Perception semantic folder is closed for the V3.1 generic core scope after completion of the canonical identity, responsibility, research synthesis, authoring structure, boundary controls, cross-layer validation, provenance, registry, index, example/entry-depth model, and lifecycle checks.

Closure of the generic core does not promote controlled candidates. Specialized perceptual concepts remain separately gated.

## Canonical Nucleus

- `SEM-PERCEPTION-GENERIC-001` — Perception — Active

## Gate A — Identity

**PASS**

Stable identity, preferred name, artifact type, semantic layer, authority, provenance, validation state, and related IDs are present in the canonical entry.

## Gate B — Responsibility

**PASS**

Primary responsibility is explicitly defined as an observer-relative perceptual phenomenon, experience, organization, appearance, or perceptual attribute.

## Gate C — Structure / Template

**PASS**

Canonical entry follows the established GIOP semantic pattern: identity, 5W1H orientation, semantic definition, distinctions, domain-specific technical depth, evidence/trust, lifecycle, relations, and retrieval anchors. Folder-level analysis, index, boundary, cross-layer, and example artifacts are also present.

## Gate D — Provenance

**PASS**

Research basis and GIOP semantic synthesis are distinguished. The registry retains evidence and candidate disposition separately from canonical semantics.

## Gate E — Claims / Technical Scope

**PASS FOR GENERIC CORE**

The generic definition and boundaries were reviewed against the assembled Perception research corpus. Specialized empirical claims remain scoped and are not promoted as universal facts.

## Gate F — Cross-References

**PASS**

The canonical entry explicitly distinguishes Observer, Observer Model, Sensation, Observation, Property, Quantity, Quantity Value, Condition, Context, State, Representation, Process, Activity, Model, Metric, and cognition-adjacent responsibilities.

## Gate G — Duplicate Authority

**PASS**

No duplicate canonical Perception root was introduced. Existing `SEM-PERCEPTION-GENERIC-001` was refined rather than replaced. Related Observer semantics remain owned by their established layers.

## Gate H — Version / Lifecycle

**PASS**

Canonical lifecycle vocabulary is preserved. Generic Perception is Active at version 1.1.0. Candidate concepts remain controlled/deferred and are not represented as active canonical identities.

## Gate I — Trust / Integrity

**PASS**

Evidence, provenance, uncertainty of scope, and limitations are visible. The implementation does not equate observed response, model output, or subjective report with universal perceptual truth.

## Gate J — Scoped Promotion / Closure

**PASS**

The V3.1 generic Perception implementation is approved for scoped canonical closure. Closure applies only to the generic core and does not authorize automatic promotion of specialized candidates.

## Visitor Universe Closure Rule

Visitor Universe has been incorporated as an entry-depth and retrieval principle only. No visitor-specific semantic variants were created. Closure of the semantic folder therefore does not create visitor-specific knowledge branches.

## Evidence Basis

The closure decision is based on the three-stage Perception research corpus and subsequent integrated semantic analysis, including CIE terminology, vision science, psychophysics, observer-model research, image-quality methodology, material perception, multisensory perception, and GIOP Foundation controls.

## Completion Rule

No additional generic Perception root is required for V3.1. Future work should proceed to specialized candidate promotion only when justified, or continue to the next semantic folder in the established sequence. Reopening the generic core requires evidence of semantic failure, material boundary conflict, or a new interoperability requirement.
