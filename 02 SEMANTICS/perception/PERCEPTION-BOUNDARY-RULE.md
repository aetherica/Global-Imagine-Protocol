# Perception Boundary Rule

**Artifact Type:** Semantic Boundary Rule  
**Semantic Layer:** Perception  
**Version:** 1.1.0  
**Status:** Active  
**Authority:** GIOP

## Governing Rule

The Perception layer owns reusable canonical semantics whose primary responsibility is an observer-relative perceptual phenomenon, experience, organization, appearance, or perceptual attribute.

It does not own the observer entity, physical stimulus, sensory response, observation activity, model used to predict response, representation used to encode evidence, or numerical result used to characterize a perceptual phenomenon.

## Hard Boundaries

- Perception ≠ Observer.
- Perception ≠ Observer Model.
- Perception ≠ Sensation.
- Perception ≠ Observation.
- Perception ≠ Property.
- Perception ≠ Quantity or Quantity Value.
- Perception ≠ State, Condition, or Context.
- Perception ≠ Representation or Dataset.
- Perception ≠ Process or Activity.
- Perception ≠ Model or Algorithm.
- Perception ≠ Metric, rating, score, or measurement result.
- Perception ≠ Recognition, understanding, reasoning, or decision.

## Experience and Consciousness Boundary

Perceptual experience may be consciously accessible, but consciousness or verbal report is not a universal identity criterion for Perception. Unreported or non-consciously accessible perceptual processing does not automatically fall outside the semantic layer.

## Sensation Boundary

Sensation and Perception are distinct semantic responsibilities. GIOP does not require a universal serial physiological pipeline from sensation to perception because scientific models and terminology vary.

## Psychophysics Boundary

Psychophysical investigation links controlled stimuli and tasks to observer responses. Thresholds, ratings, response times, sensitivity measures, discrimination results, and derived metrics are evidence or characterization outputs.

`Psychophysical Response ≠ Perception`  
`Measurement Result ≠ Perception`  
`Metric ≠ Perception`

## Appearance and Attribute Boundary

Perceptual appearance and perceptual attributes belong within the Perception semantic domain when their primary responsibility is how an entity or stimulus is perceptually experienced. Their physical counterparts remain in Property/Quantity layers when those layers own the primary responsibility.

Example:

`Luminance → Quantity`  
`Brightness → Perceptual Attribute`  
`Brightness Rating → Response / Value / Result`

## Cognition Boundary

Perception may interact with cognition, recognition, categorization, understanding, reasoning, decision, and action. Interaction does not imply semantic identity. Cognitive concepts require independent responsibility analysis.

## Model Boundary

Standard Observer, Ideal Observer, Computational Observer, and other observer models are formal semantic constructs that may specify or predict response behaviour. They are not Perception itself.

`MODELED ≠ EXPERIENCED`

## Colorimetric Boundary

CIE standard observers, colour-matching functions, colour appearance models, and colourimetric calculations may characterize or model observer-related perceptual phenomena. They must not be conflated with generic Perception.

## Context and Condition Boundary

Observer, stimulus, task, adaptation, illumination, surround, viewing geometry, spatial field, temporal regime, expectation, prior experience, Context, and Condition may qualify a perceptual assertion. They become separate canonical concepts only when independent semantic responsibility is established.

## Canonicalization Rule

A candidate is eligible for Perception only if its primary responsibility is a reusable perceptual phenomenon, experience, organization, appearance, or perceptual attribute and it passes evidence, identity, boundary, provenance, lifecycle, and promotion controls.

## Visitor Neutrality

Visitor Universe categories influence access depth, orientation, navigation, and retrieval only. They never determine the semantic identity of a perceptual concept and must not create visitor-specific canonical variants.
