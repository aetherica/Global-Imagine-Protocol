## 5W1H Orientation

### What
Fabric is a textile structure formed from fibres, yarns, filaments, or related constituents through a defined construction mechanism.

### Why
Fabric is the principal structural textile substrate used in many garments, technical textiles, and other textile articles.

### Who
Designers, textile engineers, manufacturers, inspectors, imaging systems, and AI models use fabric semantics.

### Where
Woven, knitted, nonwoven, felted, bonded, layered, and other textile constructions.

### When
Fabric structure and properties may change through finishing, cutting, sewing, laundering, wear, deformation, or damage.

### How
Fabric is characterized by construction, constituent arrangement, surface, dimensions, and relevant measured properties.

## Semantic Definition
**Fabric** is a constructed textile structure whose organization of fibres, yarns, filaments, loops, layers, or related elements forms a coherent textile material or article component.

## Scope / Boundary
Fabric construction method belongs to Process/Activity; fibre/yarn composition belongs to Textile/Material; measured properties belong to Quantity/Property.

## Core Distinctions
**Fabric vs Textile:** fabric is a specific constructed textile structure; textile is broader.

**Woven vs Knit vs Nonwoven:** construction categories, not separate root semantic layers.

**Fabric vs Garment:** substrate/component versus assembled wearable article.

## Cross-Domain Significance
Connects Textile, Material, Garment, Surface, Property, Quantity, Process, Representation, and BIL.

## Trust / Evidence
Construction claims should identify the observation/measurement basis where visually similar structures can be ambiguous.
