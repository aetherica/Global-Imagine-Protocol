## 5W1H Orientation

### What
Textile is the domain of fibrous, yarn-based, fabric, and related textile structures and the products or materials characterized by them. It encompasses textile-forming structures without collapsing all textile semantics into a single material category.

### Why
A textile domain is needed to distinguish fibre, yarn, fabric, construction, finishing, and textile-specific characteristics from generic material, property, quantity, and process semantics.

### Who
Textile semantics are relevant to manufacturers, designers, engineers, laboratories, quality systems, retailers, conservators, imaging systems, and users interpreting textile products.

### Where
Textile structures occur in fabrics, garments, technical textiles, household products, industrial materials, and other fibrous assemblies. Textile context can coexist with garment and generic material semantics.

### When
Textile identity and structure may change through manufacture, finishing, wear, laundering, damage, aging, or transformation. Temporal changes should be represented through State, Process, or Temporal semantics as appropriate.

### How
Textiles are characterized through fibre composition, yarn construction, fabric structure, finishing, measurable properties, visual/physical representations, and manufacturing history. Domain-specific measurements remain owned by Quantity/Property layers.

## Semantic Definition
Textile is the domain semantic construct covering fibrous and textile-forming materials and structures, including fibre, yarn, fabric, and textile-specific construction or finishing characteristics.

## Scope / Boundary
Textile owns domain vocabulary and structural distinctions. Material owns generic material semantics; Property and Quantity own characteristics and measurements; Process owns manufacturing/finishing operations; Garment owns assembled wearable articles. Textile does not become a second ontology for these layers.

## Core Distinctions
- Textile ≠ generic Material.
- Fibre ≠ Yarn.
- Yarn ≠ Fabric.
- Fabric ≠ Garment.
- Textile structure ≠ textile property.

## Cross-Domain Significance
Textile semantics connect materials science, manufacturing, garment construction, imaging, tactile/physical characterization, care, and product representation. Textile structure often determines observable visual and mechanical behavior.

## Trust / Evidence
Textile claims may rely on composition analysis, microscopy, structural inspection, manufacturing records, labels, provenance, or laboratory measurements. Evidence quality depends on method and sample representativeness.

## Visitor Universe
Novices need the fibre→yarn→fabric hierarchy. Experts need construction, finishing, composition, morphology, performance properties, and process history. Machines need typed textile entities and links to material, property, quantity, process, and representation concepts.

## Lifecycle
Fibre formation → yarn formation → fabric formation → finishing → conversion/use → care/wear → degradation or recycling. Not every textile follows every stage.
