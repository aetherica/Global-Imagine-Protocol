# Scene

**GIOP Semantic Class ID:** `SEM-CLASS-SCENE-001`

## Identity

**Preferred Name:** Scene  
**Artifact Class:** Structured Contextual Entity or Environment  
**Artifact Type:** Scene  
**Primary Domain:** Imaging, observation, and spatial context  

## 5W1H Orientation

**What:** A scene is a contextually bounded configuration of entities, spatial relationships, conditions, and relevant environmental or observational circumstances treated as a coherent imaging or observation target.

**Why:** Scene provides the contextual whole within which objects, surfaces, illumination, observation, and imaging relationships can be described.

**Who:** A scene may be defined or analyzed by photographers, cinematographers, scientists, computer-vision systems, simulators, archivists, or other observers.

**Where:** A scene may be physical, simulated, virtual, rendered, recorded, or mixed, depending on the domain.

**When:** A scene is associated with a defined temporal scope. The scene may change over time even when the scene identity remains useful.

**How:** A scene groups relevant entities and contextual relationships for a specified task, viewpoint, interval, or domain.

## Semantic Definition

A **Scene** is a contextually bounded configuration of entities and relationships treated as a coherent target or environment for imaging, observation, analysis, or representation.

Scene is deliberately contextual rather than a claim that every scene constitutes a universal physical ontology.

## Principal Types

Examples include:

- physical scene;
- photographic scene;
- cinematic scene;
- scientific imaging scene;
- machine-vision scene;
- simulated scene;
- rendered scene;
- virtual scene;
- mixed-reality scene;
- reconstructed scene;
- remote-sensing scene.

## Distinctions

### Scene vs Image

A scene is the target or contextual configuration. An image is a representation produced from or associated with a scene.

### Scene vs Object

A scene may contain or contextualize one or more objects. An object is an identifiable entity within a relevant domain.

### Scene vs Environment

Environment may describe surrounding physical or contextual circumstances. Scene is a bounded configuration treated as an imaging or observation target.

### Scene vs Representation

A scene may be represented by an image, video, 3D model, dataset, or other representation. The representation is not the scene itself.

## Scope

A scene may include, as relevant:

- objects;
- surfaces;
- materials;
- illumination;
- light sources;
- spatial arrangement;
- camera viewpoint;
- observer viewpoint;
- environmental optical conditions;
- temporal context;
- simulated or virtual entities.

The included scope depends on the task and should be explicitly bounded when precision matters.

## Technical Characteristics

Relevant scene characteristics may include:

- spatial extent;
- viewpoint;
- composition;
- geometry;
- depth structure;
- illumination configuration;
- temporal interval;
- spectral context;
- object and surface arrangement;
- environmental context;
- simulation state where applicable.

## Trust and Validation

Scene descriptions should distinguish observed facts from inferred or reconstructed content. A scene inferred from an image is not necessarily identical to the unknown physical scene that generated the image.

Evidence may include:

- direct observation;
- calibrated imaging;
- sensor data;
- multiple viewpoints;
- measurement records;
- simulation parameters;
- provenance and capture metadata.

## Lifecycle

`Formation / Selection → Observation or Construction → Capture / Representation → Analysis → Modification / Evolution → Archival or Retirement`

## Relations

A scene may:

- contain objects;
- contain or expose surfaces;
- include materials;
- be illuminated by light sources;
- be observed by an observer;
- be captured by a camera;
- be measured by a measuring system;
- generate or correspond to representations;
- change over time.

## Machine and AI Interpretation

A machine should treat scene membership and scene reconstruction as distinct. A representation may provide evidence about a scene without proving every physical property of the scene.

## Retrieval Anchors

Primary terms: `scene`, `imaging scene`, `observation scene`, `visual scene`.

Related terms: `physical scene`, `virtual scene`, `simulated scene`, `rendered scene`, `machine-vision scene`.

## Semantic Boundary

This class establishes **what a scene is as a contextual entity**. It does not define image content, object identity, environmental conditions, physical measurements, camera parameters, or perceptual interpretation.
