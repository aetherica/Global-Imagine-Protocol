# Material

**GIOP Semantic Class ID:** `SEM-CLASS-MATERIAL-001`

## Identity

**Preferred Name:** Material  
**Artifact Class:** Physical Substance or Medium  
**Artifact Type:** Material  
**Primary Domain:** Physical and optical matter  

## 5W1H Orientation

**What:** A material is a physical substance or medium identified by its composition, structure, or domain-relevant physical identity.

**Why:** Material identity provides the physical basis for properties and interactions relevant to imaging, optics, manufacturing, measurement, and observation.

**Who:** Material concepts are used by material scientists, optical engineers, manufacturers, metrologists, imaging professionals, and computational systems.

**Where:** Materials occur in objects, surfaces, optical components, sensors, environments, and manufactured or natural systems.

**When:** Material behavior may depend on wavelength, temperature, structure, processing, environmental conditions, and physical state.

**How:** A material interacts with radiation, matter, and other physical systems according to its composition and structure.

## Semantic Definition

A **Material** is a physical substance or medium recognized as an entity for purposes of composition, structure, manufacture, use, or domain-relevant behavior.

Material identity is distinct from the identity of an object made from that material and from the identity of a surface exhibiting material-dependent behavior.

## Distinctions

### Material vs Surface

Material identifies what a physical substance or medium is. Surface identifies a spatial boundary or interface associated with it.

### Material vs Object

An object is an identifiable entity that may be composed of one or more materials. A material may occur in many different objects.

### Material vs Appearance

Appearance describes how an entity is presented or perceived under specified conditions. Material identifies the physical substance or medium.

### Material vs Texture

Texture may describe a physical or perceptual characteristic. It does not by itself establish material identity.

## Principal Types

Examples include:

- glass;
- optical polymer;
- semiconductor material;
- metal;
- ceramic;
- coating material;
- textile material;
- paper;
- biological material;
- composite material;
- photoluminescent material;
- fluorescent material;
- translucent or transparent material.

These examples are domain-oriented and are not an exhaustive material taxonomy.

## Optical Characteristics

Relevant material characteristics may include:

- refractive behavior;
- transmission;
- absorption;
- reflection;
- scattering;
- emission;
- fluorescence;
- photoluminescence;
- spectral behavior;
- polarization response;
- dispersion;
- surface interaction;
- environmental dependence.

These are properties or quantities and must not be conflated with material identity.

## Trust and Validation

Material claims should be supported by appropriate evidence, such as:

- composition records;
- manufacturer or supplier documentation;
- material specifications;
- laboratory characterization;
- spectroscopy or other analytical methods;
- standardized testing;
- traceable measurement where required.

Visual appearance alone does not establish material composition.

## Lifecycle

`Source / Formation → Processing → Manufacture → Finishing → Use → Aging / Environmental Change → Recycling / Reuse / Disposal`

## Relations

A material may:

- compose an object;
- constitute a surface;
- form an optical component;
- interact with radiation;
- be characterized by measurable quantities;
- exhibit condition-dependent behavior;
- be altered by manufacturing or environmental processes.

## Machine and AI Interpretation

A machine should not infer material identity from appearance alone unless the inference is explicitly classified as an inference and supported by evidence. Material, surface, object, and representation remain distinct semantic entities.

## Retrieval Anchors

Primary terms: `material`, `substance`, `medium`, `material type`.

Related terms: `optical material`, `glass`, `polymer`, `semiconductor`, `coating`, `composite material`.

## Semantic Boundary

This class establishes **what a material is as a physical entity**. It does not define surface identity, appearance, material properties as such, measurement results, manufacturing processes, or representation content.
