# Light Source

**GIOP Semantic Class ID:** `SEM-CLASS-LIGHT-SOURCE-001`

## Identity

**Preferred Name:** Light Source  
**Artifact Class:** Radiative Source  
**Artifact Type:** Light Source  
**Primary Domain:** Optical radiation and illumination  

## 5W1H Orientation

**What:** A light source is a physical or otherwise explicitly identified radiative entity that emits or provides optical radiation relevant to an observation, illumination, imaging, or measurement context.

**Why:** A light source provides radiation that can illuminate a region, interact with an object or surface, or provide an optical input to a system.

**Who:** A light source may be natural, engineered, laboratory-controlled, environmental, or integrated into an imaging or measurement system.

**Where:** Sources occur in natural environments, photography, cinematography, microscopy, spectroscopy, machine vision, displays, laboratories, industrial systems, and scientific instruments.

**When:** Source behavior may vary with time, operating state, modulation, temperature, power, environment, and control configuration.

**How:** A source emits or otherwise provides radiation with spatial, spectral, angular, and temporal characteristics.

## Semantic Definition

A **Light Source** is a radiative entity that emits or provides optical radiation for a specified context. The source may be characterized as primary or secondary according to the relevant optical terminology and physical role.

The term **light** is used in its domain-appropriate optical sense and must not automatically be restricted to human-visible radiation when the surrounding semantic context explicitly includes other optical wavelengths.

## Source Categories

Examples include:

- natural source;
- artificial source;
- primary source;
- secondary source;
- thermal emitter;
- LED source;
- laser source;
- lamp;
- flash source;
- solar radiation source;
- display or emitting surface when acting as a source;
- specialized laboratory or scientific source.

The categories are contextual and may overlap.

## Distinctions

### Light Source vs Light Condition

A light source is an entity that emits or provides radiation. Light Condition describes whether relevant radiation is present or available in a context.

### Light Source vs Illumination Condition

The source is the origin or provider of radiation. Illumination Condition describes radiation acting on a defined receiving region, surface, object, or scene.

### Light Source vs Brightness

Brightness is a context-dependent perceptual or descriptive concept. It is not the identity of a radiative source.

### Light Source vs Radiance

Radiance is a radiometric quantity. A source may have radiance, but radiance is not a source entity.

## Technical Characteristics

Relevant characteristics may include:

- spectral distribution;
- emitted power;
- radiometric output;
- angular distribution;
- spatial distribution;
- temporal behavior;
- modulation;
- polarization;
- coherence;
- stability;
- source geometry;
- colorimetric characteristics where applicable;
- operating condition;
- environmental dependence.

These are properties or quantities and must not be conflated with source identity.

## Trust and Validation

Source claims should be supported by suitable evidence, including:

- manufacturer specifications;
- calibrated source measurements;
- laboratory characterization;
- standardized test methods;
- experimental records;
- traceable measurement data where required.

A source designation does not establish a numerical output, spectral distribution, stability, or calibration status without evidence.

## Lifecycle

`Design / Origin → Manufacture or Formation → Configuration → Characterization → Deployment → Operation → Maintenance / Aging → Retirement or Change`

## Relations

A light source may:

- illuminate a surface;
- contribute to a scene;
- provide radiation to a camera or sensor;
- interact with material;
- be observed directly or indirectly;
- participate in measurement;
- be controlled by a system or operator;
- be characterized by radiometric or colorimetric quantities.

## Machine and AI Interpretation

A machine should interpret **Light Source** as a radiative entity and should not infer visible-spectrum emission, human visibility, uniform illumination, spectral content, intensity, calibration, or temporal stability unless explicitly represented and supported.

## Retrieval Anchors

Primary terms: `light source`, `optical source`, `radiative source`, `emitter`.

Related terms: `primary source`, `secondary source`, `lamp`, `LED`, `laser`, `illuminant`.

## Semantic Boundary

This class establishes **what a light source is as a radiative entity**. It does not define illumination conditions, exposure, radiometric quantities, perceptual brightness, source performance claims, or individual source identity records.
