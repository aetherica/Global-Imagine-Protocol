# Representation

**GIOP Semantic Class ID:** `SEM-CLASS-REPRESENTATION-001`

## Identity

**Preferred Name:** Representation  
**Artifact Class:** Information-Bearing Representational Entity  
**Artifact Type:** Representation  
**Primary Domain:** Information, imaging, and communication  

## 5W1H Orientation

**What:** A representation is an information-bearing entity that stands for, describes, encodes, depicts, records, or otherwise expresses another entity, phenomenon, state, observation, or information content within a defined context.

**Why:** Representation allows information to persist, communicate, process, compare, transmit, render, archive, or be interpreted.

**Who:** Representations may be created, transformed, interpreted, stored, transmitted, or consumed by humans, machines, software, instruments, organizations, or automated systems.

**Where:** Representations occur in images, video, documents, datasets, metadata, signals, 3D models, scientific records, encoded data, and other information forms.

**When:** A representation is associated with a creation, capture, transformation, transmission, or observation context and may have multiple versions or derived forms.

**How:** A representation encodes or expresses information through a defined representational structure, medium, encoding, or modality.

## Semantic Definition

A **Representation** is an information-bearing entity that expresses or stands for another entity, phenomenon, state, observation, or information content in a defined context.

Representation is deliberately broader than **image**. An image is one possible representation; it is not the semantic definition of representation itself.

## Representation Families

Examples include:

- image representation;
- video representation;
- audio representation;
- textual representation;
- tabular representation;
- numerical representation;
- metadata representation;
- signal representation;
- 3D geometric representation;
- volumetric representation;
- dataset representation;
- symbolic representation;
- encoded digital representation.

The families are illustrative and may overlap.

## Distinctions

### Representation vs Represented Entity

A representation expresses or stands for an entity. The represented entity and the representation are distinct semantic entities.

### Representation vs Image

An image is a representation with imaging-specific semantics. Representation is the broader class.

### Representation vs File

A file is a storage or container artifact. A representation may be stored in a file, but file identity and representational meaning are distinct.

### Representation vs Encoding

Encoding is a method or structure for expressing information. Representation is the information-bearing entity expressed through an appropriate representational form.

### Representation vs Rendering

Rendering is an activity or transformation that may produce a representation. It is not the representation itself.

## Imaging Scope

An imaging representation may express information about:

- a scene;
- an object;
- a surface;
- an observation;
- a measurement;
- a physical or virtual environment;
- a derived computational result.

The representation should not be assumed to be a direct or complete copy of the represented entity.

## Technical Characteristics

Relevant characteristics may include:

- dimensionality;
- spatial structure;
- temporal structure;
- spectral structure;
- sampling;
- precision;
- encoding;
- compression;
- metadata;
- provenance;
- derivation history;
- fidelity;
- resolution;
- color or modality information.

These are properties, quantities, states, processes, or provenance information associated with a representation.

## Trust and Provenance

A representation should preserve, where available:

- source identity;
- capture or creation context;
- transformation history;
- processing operations;
- encoding information;
- timestamps;
- measurement context;
- provenance evidence.

A representation does not automatically prove that every attribute it appears to depict is physically true.

## Lifecycle

`Creation / Capture → Encoding → Storage → Transmission → Processing / Transformation → Rendering / Interpretation → Archival → Supersession or Disposal`

Multiple representations may derive from the same source entity, and one representation may be transformed into another.

## Relations

A representation may:

- represent a scene;
- depict an object or surface;
- record an observation;
- encode a measurement result;
- be generated by a camera;
- be transformed by a process;
- be stored in a file;
- be decoded or rendered;
- be interpreted by an observer or machine;
- derive from another representation.

## Machine and AI Interpretation

A machine should preserve the distinction between representation and represented entity. Visual or encoded content should not be treated as direct physical truth without considering provenance, capture conditions, transformations, and evidential status.

For AI systems, representations should be treated as information-bearing inputs or outputs whose provenance and transformation history may materially affect interpretation.

## Retrieval Anchors

Primary terms: `representation`, `information representation`, `representational entity`.

Related terms: `image`, `video`, `dataset`, `signal`, `3D representation`, `digital representation`, `encoded representation`.

## Semantic Boundary

This class establishes **what a representation is as an information-bearing entity**. It does not define specific file formats, encoding algorithms, images, rendering activities, represented physical entities, fidelity metrics, or transformation procedures.
