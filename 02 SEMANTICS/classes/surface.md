# Surface

**GIOP Semantic Class ID:** `SEM-CLASS-SURFACE-001`

## Identity

**Preferred Name:** Surface  
**Artifact Class:** Spatial Boundary or Interface Region  
**Artifact Type:** Surface  
**Primary Domain:** Physical and optical interaction  

## 5W1H Orientation

**What:** A surface is a spatially defined boundary or interface region associated with an object, material, medium, or environment.

**Why:** A surface is often the location at which radiation, matter, observation, or other interactions occur between adjacent regions or media.

**Who:** Surface properties may be relevant to imaging professionals, optical scientists, material scientists, metrologists, engineers, and computational systems.

**Where:** Surfaces occur on physical objects, materials, interfaces, optical components, natural environments, manufactured products, and other spatial entities.

**When:** Surface behavior may depend on wavelength, direction, incidence, geometry, environment, temporal state, and measurement configuration.

**How:** A surface defines a spatial interaction boundary and may reflect, transmit, absorb, scatter, emit, or otherwise interact with relevant radiation or matter.

## Semantic Definition

A **Surface** is a spatially identifiable boundary or interface region associated with an entity, material, object, or medium. In imaging and optical contexts, it is commonly the region from which or through which optical interactions relevant to observation or measurement occur.

## Distinctions

### Surface vs Material

A material identifies a physical substance or medium. A surface identifies a spatial boundary or interface associated with an entity or material.

### Surface vs Object

An object is an identifiable entity in a context. A surface is a spatial region or boundary associated with that entity.

### Surface vs Image Region

An image region is part of a representation. A surface may be represented by an image but is not itself an image region.

### Surface vs Boundary

Boundary is a broader spatial or semantic concept. Surface is a specific spatial interface or boundary region with its own domain meaning.

## Optical Role

A surface may participate in:

`Radiation → Surface Interaction → Reflection / Transmission / Absorption / Scattering / Emission → Observation or Measurement`

The exact interaction depends on the surface, material, radiation, geometry, and context.

## Technical Characteristics

Relevant characteristics may include:

- geometry;
- orientation;
- curvature;
- roughness;
- reflectance;
- transmittance;
- absorptance;
- scattering behavior;
- spectral behavior;
- directional behavior;
- spatial uniformity;
- texture;
- contamination or surface condition;
- environmental state.

These characteristics are represented through properties, quantities, conditions, or states as appropriate.

## Trust and Validation

Surface claims may require:

- controlled observation;
- material characterization;
- optical measurement;
- surface metrology;
- calibrated imaging;
- standardized test methods;
- documented environmental conditions.

An image appearance alone does not necessarily establish a physical surface property.

## Lifecycle

`Formation / Manufacture → Finishing → Inspection → Use → Environmental Change / Wear → Maintenance → Alteration or Retirement`

## Relations

A surface may:

- belong to an object;
- be associated with a material;
- receive illumination;
- reflect or transmit radiation;
- be observed by a camera, sensor, or observer;
- be measured;
- be represented in an image;
- exhibit texture or appearance.

## Machine and AI Interpretation

A machine should not infer a physical surface property solely from a visual representation unless the relevant inference is explicitly stated and supported. A represented image region and the physical surface it depicts are distinct entities.

## Semantic Boundary

This class establishes **what a surface is as a spatial entity**. It does not define material identity, surface measurements, optical quantities, appearance, image regions, or representation content.
