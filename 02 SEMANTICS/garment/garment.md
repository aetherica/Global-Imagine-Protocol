## 5W1H Orientation

### What
A Garment is an assembled wearable article designed to cover, support, protect, or otherwise interface with part or all of a body.

### Why
Garment semantics are required to distinguish the assembled wearable article from the textile materials from which it may be made and from the body it is intended to fit.

### Who
Applicable to apparel, costume, protective clothing, sportswear, uniforms, adaptive garments, virtual garments, and machine-readable clothing representations.

### Where
Physical garments, product catalogues, images, video, 3D clothing models, design systems, and e-commerce records.

### When
Garment identity and state can change through alteration, wear, damage, laundering, folding, donning, doffing, or reconstruction.

### How
A garment is characterized by article type, components, construction, intended use, size/fit context, closures, materials, and representation.

## Semantic Definition
**Garment** is an assembled wearable article intended to interact with and cover/support a body or body region under a declared use context.

## Scope / Boundary
Garment does not own textile constituent semantics, generic material semantics, body anatomy, anthropometric quantities, or generic representation. It also does not own manufacturing or wearing operations.

## Core Distinctions
Garment vs Textile: assembled wearable article vs material/structure.

Garment vs Accessory: garments are wearable articles with garment construction; accessories may accompany or attach without constituting the garment.

Garment vs Garment Representation: the physical/logical article is distinct from its image, pattern, mesh, drawing, or record.

## Cross-Domain Significance
Connects Textile, Material, Classes, Properties, States, Activities, Representations, Quantities, and BIL.

## Trust / Evidence
Article type, component structure, fit context, and representation modality should be explicit where automated systems infer garment semantics from imagery.
