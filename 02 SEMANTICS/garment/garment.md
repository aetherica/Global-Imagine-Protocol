## 5W1H Orientation

### What
A Garment is an assembled wearable article or wearable article system intended to cover, clothe, protect, support, or otherwise interact with a body or wearer according to a defined design and use context.

### Why
Garment semantics are needed to distinguish the complete wearable article from its textile/material constituents, construction components, patterns, size designations, fit, and fastening mechanisms.

### Who
Garment semantics serve designers, pattern makers, manufacturers, technologists, retailers, users, inspectors, conservators, imaging systems, and virtual garment or fitting systems.

### Where
Garments exist as physical wearable articles and can also be represented in images, video, CAD, simulation, catalogues, and digital product systems. Representation remains owned by the Representation layer.

### When
A garment has lifecycle stages from design and construction through fitting, use, care, repair, alteration, degradation, reuse, and disposal. Its state may change while garment identity persists.

### How
A garment is defined by its intended article identity, components, construction, pattern, textile/material constituents, size designation, fit, fastening, and relevant state. Measurements and properties remain owned by Quantity/Property.

## Semantic Definition
Garment is an assembled wearable article whose identity is defined by its intended wearable configuration, components, construction, and relevant design characteristics.

## Scope / Boundary
Garment owns wearable-article semantics. Textile owns textile structures/materials; Material owns generic material semantics; Quantity owns measurements; Property owns characteristics; Pattern and Construction describe garment-specific organization; BIL evaluates garment-related integrity when relevant but does not own garment semantics.

## Core Distinctions
- Garment ≠ Textile.
- Garment ≠ fabric.
- Garment ≠ pattern.
- Garment size designation ≠ body measurement.
- Garment fit ≠ garment identity.

## Cross-Domain Significance
Garment semantics connect textile materials, body/anthropometric measurements, construction, fit, interaction, representation, care, and visual integrity. Garment behavior depends on both construction and wearer context.

## Trust / Evidence
Evidence may include physical inspection, construction records, labels, product documentation, measurements, images, and provenance. A visual representation alone may be insufficient for hidden construction claims.

## Visitor Universe
Novices need garment-versus-textile distinction. Experts need component structure, construction, sizing, fit, fastening, materials, and lifecycle. Machines need typed garment entities and links to components, textiles, measurements, representations, and state.

## Lifecycle
Design → pattern development → material selection → construction → fitting/quality control → use → care/repair/alteration → reuse/recycling/disposal.
