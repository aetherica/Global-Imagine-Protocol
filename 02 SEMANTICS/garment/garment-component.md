# Garment Component

## 5W1H Orientation

### What
A Garment Component is a semantically distinguishable part or functional element of a garment that participates in the garment's assembled configuration.

### Why
Component semantics allow a garment to be described compositionally, so sleeves, collars, panels, linings, pockets, closures, and other parts can be represented without treating the entire garment as an indivisible object.

### Who
Designers, pattern makers, manufacturers, inspectors, digital garment systems, and analysts use component semantics to describe and reason about garment structure.

### Where
Components occur within an assembled garment and may correspond to pattern pieces, construction units, functional elements, or material-bearing regions.

### When
Components arise during design/construction and may be added, removed, altered, replaced, damaged, or repaired during the garment lifecycle.

### How
A component is identified by its role, boundaries, relation to the garment and other components, construction participation, and relevant material/textile constituents.

## Semantic Definition
Garment Component is a distinguishable constituent of a garment that contributes structurally, functionally, or materially to the assembled wearable article.

## Scope / Boundary
Component owns garment-specific part semantics. Generic Part-of remains a Relation-layer concept. Pattern owns pattern artifacts; Construction owns assembly semantics; Textile/Material own constituents.

## Core Distinctions
- Component ≠ pattern piece, although a component may be produced from one or more pattern pieces.
- Component ≠ material.
- Component ≠ garment itself.
- Component boundary ≠ arbitrary image region.

## Cross-Domain Significance
Components connect garment construction with textile/material composition, fit, fastening, repair, imaging, and digital representation.

## Trust / Evidence
Component claims can be supported by construction documentation, physical inspection, technical drawings, images, or digital garment models. Occluded components may require non-visual evidence.

## Visitor Universe
Novices need whole-garment-versus-part distinction. Experts need hierarchical components, construction dependency, material mapping, and hidden components. Machines need typed part-of relations and component roles.

## Lifecycle
Design → cutting/preparation → assembly → inspection → use → alteration/repair → replacement or disassembly.
