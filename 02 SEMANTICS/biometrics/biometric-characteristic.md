## 5W1H Orientation

### What
A biometric characteristic is a measurable biological or behavioural characteristic used as the basis for biometric recognition or comparison.

### Why
Biometric systems require characteristics that can be acquired and represented in a manner suitable for comparison.

### Who
Applicable to human and other biological subjects where a defined biometric application exists; the application scope must be declared.

### Where
Physical observation, sensor acquisition, biometric datasets, recognition systems, and forensic or authentication contexts.

### When
A characteristic may vary over time, context, health, behaviour, sensor conditions, and acquisition conditions.

### How
A characteristic is acquired as data, transformed into features or templates, and used by a biometric process.

## Semantic Definition
**Biometric Characteristic** is a biological or behavioural characteristic whose measurable manifestation is used for biometric recognition or comparison within a specified application.

## Core Distinctions
Characteristic ≠ Sample: characteristic is the underlying biometric phenomenon; sample is an acquired representation.

Characteristic ≠ Modality: modality classifies the biometric technique/characteristic family.

Characteristic ≠ Identity: it provides evidence for identity-related decisions but does not itself constitute identity.

## Trust / Evidence
The application should state subject population, acquisition conditions, variability assumptions, and intended biometric task.

## Cross-Domain Significance
Routes to Property, Quantity, Observation, Measurement, Representation, Identity, and Process without redefining them.
