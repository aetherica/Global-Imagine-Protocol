# GIOP Domain Semantic Authoring Standard

**Status:** CANONICAL AUTHORING STANDARD — V3.1
**Purpose:** Apply one substantive authoring, evidence, boundary, visitor, lifecycle, and retrieval protocol across domain-semantic folders.

## Governing Principle

Domain folders may differ in subject matter, but their canonical dataset entries must be authored at comparable semantic depth. A heading without substantive content does not satisfy the protocol.

The standard is derived from the mature Condition authoring pattern and adapted only where a domain concept requires different dimensions.

## Required Entry Structure

Every substantive domain entry must contain, at minimum:

1. Identity / Metadata
2. 5W1H Orientation
3. Semantic Definition
4. Scope / Boundary
5. Core Distinctions
6. Context / Dimensions where semantically applicable
7. Cross-Domain Significance
8. Trust / Evidence
9. Visitor Universe
10. Lifecycle
11. Retrieval Anchors

## 5W1H Depth Protocol

Each of What, Why, Who, Where, When, and How must contain concept-specific prose. The section must explain the semantic role of the concept rather than repeat its name or provide a generic sentence.

- What: identify the entity, phenomenon, relation, state, role, or domain construct.
- Why: explain its semantic and practical purpose.
- Who: identify relevant agents, systems, populations, or stakeholders.
- Where: define spatial, organizational, representational, or domain scope where applicable.
- When: explain temporal applicability, persistence, change, or lifecycle relevance.
- How: explain how the concept is formed, observed, represented, measured, interpreted, or used without replacing Process/Method semantics.

Not every concept requires identical paragraph length, but every field must be substantively answered or explicitly marked not semantically applicable with justification.

## Definition Depth Protocol

The Semantic Definition must state the genus/role and the distinguishing characteristics. It must not merely paraphrase the title.

## Boundary Protocol

Every entry must state what the concept owns and what nearby concepts/layers own. Duplication with generic GIOP layers is prohibited merely for convenience.

## Distinction Protocol

At least the principal confusions must be explicitly separated. Examples include identity versus identifier, fibre versus yarn, fabric versus garment, biometric sample versus template, size designation versus measurement, and representation versus referent.

## Evidence and Trust Protocol

Evidence must be distinguished from truth, provenance, authenticity, confidence, and decision. Where evidence can be incomplete or conflicting, the entry must preserve that distinction.

## Visitor Universe Protocol

Entries must explain how novice, expert, and machine visitors enter the same canonical concept at different depths. Visitor Universe is an orientation/routing mechanism, not duplicate content.

## Lifecycle Protocol

Describe the concept's relevant creation, use, change, validation, retirement, or transformation trajectory. Do not invent lifecycle stages when they are not semantically meaningful.

## Retrieval Protocol

Retrieval anchors must include stable domain terminology and important synonyms without turning the entry into an uncontrolled keyword dump.

## Standardization Rule

Same pattern and same depth protocol do not mean identical prose. Domain-specific semantics determine the content of each field; the authoring architecture remains consistent.

## Completion Rule

A folder is implementation-complete only when its substantive entries satisfy this standard, its scope is explicitly bounded, its cross-layer routing is recorded, its dataset/index/audit are synchronized, and canonical promotion remains separately governed by validation and Gate-J.
