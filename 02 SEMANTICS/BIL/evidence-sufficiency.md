# BIL Evidence Sufficiency

**Domain:** Biological / Configurational Integrity  
**Status:** Candidate  
**Version:** 0.2.0

> **Evidence Sufficiency** is the scoped determination of whether available evidence is adequate to support or evaluate a particular Integrity Claim.

## 5W1H Orientation

### What
A claim-specific assessment of whether the available evidence is adequate for the intended integrity question.

### Why
A reference may be valid but incomplete, or a visible frame may be insufficient for hidden anatomy, gait, group attribution or temporal continuity. Insufficiency must not be confused with failure.

### Who
Relevant to validation engineers, imaging scientists, researchers, VFX teams, wildlife specialists, auditors and AI systems.

### Where
Still, video, multi-view, scientific, wildlife, sports, dance, VFX and 3D/4D evaluation contexts.

### When
Determined before or during Integrity Validation for a specific claim and decision policy.

### How
Consider claim scope, reference quality, observability, coverage, evidence independence, uncertainty, occlusion, temporal coverage, representation limits, provenance and conflicts.

## Semantic Definition

**Evidence Sufficiency** is the BIL control concept denoting whether available evidence adequately supports evaluation of a declared Integrity Claim at its applicable scope and decision policy.

## Core Distinctions

Sufficiency ≠ confidence; ≠ completeness; ≠ truth; ≠ validation success; ≠ universal threshold.

## Scope and Boundary

Sufficiency is always claim-specific. A source may be sufficient for facial identity but insufficient for gait, hidden anatomy or multi-view geometry.

## Cross-Domain Significance

Sufficiency consumes reference, representation, observation, measurement, temporal and provenance information from existing GIOP layers.

## Trust and Evidence

Rationale, evidence coverage and uncertainty must be retained. Thresholds are profile or validation artifacts unless separately promoted.
