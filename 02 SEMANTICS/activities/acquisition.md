## 5W1H Orientation

### What

Acquisition is an Activity in which information or data is obtained or captured from a defined source, scene, object, phenomenon, or operational context.

### Why

Acquisition represents the actual capture/obtaining event and separates that event from the Camera, Sensor, Lens, Measuring System, image/data product, and acquisition Procedure that may participate in it.

### Who

Acquisition may be performed by a Camera, Sensor, Measuring System, computational system, human-operated setup, or coordinated system, depending on the domain.

### Where

Acquisition may occur in photography, imaging science, machine vision, remote sensing, laboratory measurement, scientific instrumentation, simulation, virtual production, or other capture contexts.

### When

Acquisition is temporally situated. Capture time, exposure interval, phenomenon time, and result availability may be different temporal facts.

### How

Acquisition uses an acquisition system or participating entities under relevant Conditions and according to a Procedure or operational configuration where applicable. It generates acquired data or an information-bearing entity.

## Semantic Definition

**Acquisition** is an Activity in which information or data is obtained or captured from a defined source, scene, object, phenomenon, or operational context.

Acquisition describes the occurrence of obtaining information; it does not denote the instrument, the optical component, the sensor, or the resulting image/data entity.

## Semantic Responsibility

Acquisition owns the capture/obtaining occurrence in an information acquisition chain.

## Core Distinctions

### Acquisition vs Camera

Camera is a system-level capture entity. Acquisition is the actual capture occurrence performed or enabled by that system.

`Acquisition ≠ Camera`.

### Acquisition vs Sensor

Sensor is a sensing entity or subsystem. Acquisition is the activity in which information is obtained using a sensing system.

`Acquisition ≠ Sensor`.

### Acquisition vs Lens

Lens is an optical component or assembly. It participates in the acquisition subsystem but is not the acquisition occurrence.

### Acquisition vs Image / Data

An image or dataset is an entity or representation generated by acquisition. It is not the acquisition activity.

### Acquisition vs Observation

Acquisition emphasizes obtaining/capturing information. Observation emphasizes the observation act and its phenomenon/property target. They may co-occur and should not be collapsed solely because a capture produces an observation result.

### Acquisition vs Measurement

Acquisition may provide input to measurement, but obtaining data does not automatically make the acquisition a measurement. Measurement requires the quantitative semantic responsibility of obtaining quantity value(s).

## Optical Acquisition Context

The established GIOP acquisition subsystem remains:

```text
Camera
   ↓
Lens
   ↓
Sensor
   ↓
Acquisition Activity
   ↓
Acquired Data / Image / Representation
```

This is a conceptual dependency, not a mandatory physical hierarchy. Specific systems may integrate or alter components.

## Conditions

Acquisition may depend on or occur under:

- Illumination Condition;
- Light Condition;
- Environmental Optical Conditions;
- Optical Condition;
- Camera configuration;
- Lens configuration;
- Sensor state/configuration;
- scene/object/material context;
- temporal conditions.

Conditions remain contextual semantic entities and are not absorbed into Acquisition.

## Temporal Semantics

Relevant temporal facts may include acquisition start/end, exposure interval, phenomenon time, trigger time, and result time. These must remain distinguishable when they describe different events.

## Inputs and Outputs

Typical inputs or participating entities include Scene, Object, Surface, Material, Light Source, Camera, Lens, Sensor, Measuring System, Conditions, States, Procedure, and acquisition parameters.

Outputs may include image data, sensor data, signal data, samples, or other acquired representations/entities.

## Cross-Domain Significance

Acquisition is a general operational concept for capture systems. It connects physical or simulated source context with information products while preserving the distinction between system architecture and operational occurrence.

## Trust / Provenance

Acquisition records should preserve the acquisition system, relevant configuration, temporal information, conditions, source/scene context, procedure or method where applicable, generated entity, evidence, validation state, and authority context.

Repository placement is not semantic authority. External standards provide evidence for the semantic synthesis but do not automatically define GIOP terminology.
