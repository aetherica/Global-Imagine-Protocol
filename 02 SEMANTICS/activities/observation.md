**Semantic Class:** Activity  
**Domain:** Observation and imaging  
**Status:** Provisional  
**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Primary Responsibility:** An observation-oriented Activity in which information about a phenomenon, feature, property, or state is obtained.

## 5W1H Orientation

### What

Observation is an Activity in which an observer, sensor, instrument, system, or other observing entity obtains information about a phenomenon or feature of interest.

### Why

Observation provides a semantic layer for the act of obtaining information without requiring the result to be a numerical quantity value. It therefore supports imaging, sensing, inspection, scientific observation, and categorical or structured outcomes.

### Who

An Observation may involve a human observer, Sensor, Camera, Measuring System, software system, or another observing System.

### Where

Observation may occur in physical scenes, laboratories, imaging systems, remote sensing, simulation, virtual environments, displays, or other observation contexts.

### When

Observation has an execution time and may concern a phenomenon occurring at a different time. Result availability may also occur later than the phenomenon and execution.

### How

An Observation follows an observation method or Procedure where applicable, involves an observing System or Agent, concerns a feature or property, and produces an observation result or other information-bearing entity.

## Semantic Definition

**Observation** is an Activity in which information about a phenomenon, feature, property, or state is obtained through an observing act.

An Observation may produce numerical, categorical, spatial, temporal, image-based, structured, or other information. It is not restricted to quantity measurement.

## Semantic Responsibility

The primary responsibility of Observation is to represent the act of obtaining or establishing information about something being observed.

## Core Distinctions

### Observation vs Measurement

Observation is broader in epistemic scope. Measurement is specifically concerned with experimentally obtaining quantity value(s) attributable to a quantity. An Observation may yield a measurement, but not every Observation is a Measurement.

`Observation ≠ Measurement`.

### Observation vs Observer

Observer identifies the observing entity or observer model. Observation identifies the occurrence of observing.

`Observer ≠ Observation`.

### Observation vs Sensor

A Sensor is an entity or system used to detect or sense. Observation is the activity/occurrence carried out using such a system.

### Observation vs Result

Observation is the act. The observation result is what is obtained or generated by that act.

### Observation vs Representation

The result may be represented as an image, table, geometry, signal, category, or other representation. The observation occurrence is not itself that representation.

## Feature, Property, and Phenomenon Context

An Observation should identify, where relevant:

- feature or object of interest;
- observed property or characteristic;
- phenomenon being observed;
- relevant Conditions and States;
- observing System or Agent;
- observation Procedure or method;
- temporal context.

The semantic identity of these concepts remains in their respective GIOP layers.

## Imaging Context

In imaging, an Observation may involve:

```text
Scene / Object
      ↓
Conditions
      ↓
Camera / Lens / Sensor
      ↓
Observation / Acquisition Activity
      ↓
Image or Observation Result
      ↓
Representation / Display
```

Observation and Acquisition may occur together, but they retain distinct semantic responsibilities: Acquisition concerns obtaining/capturing information; Observation concerns the observation act and its epistemic target.

## Temporal Semantics

Relevant times may include:

- execution/observation start and end;
- phenomenon time;
- result time.

Phenomenon time must not be assumed to equal execution time or result time.

## Inputs and Outputs

Typical inputs or participating entities may include Scene, Object, Surface, Light Source, Camera, Sensor, Measuring System, Procedure, Conditions, and States.

Outputs may include an Observation Result, image, signal, categorical statement, geometry, measurement value, or other information-bearing entity.

## Cross-Domain Significance

Observation is a general bridge between physical or simulated phenomena and information. It supports optical imaging, scientific observation, machine vision, inspection, remote sensing, experimental work, and computational observation systems.

## Trust / Provenance

An observation claim should preserve the observing system or agent, procedure or method where applicable, temporal context, phenomenon/feature context, generated result, source/evidence status, validation state, and relevant conditions.

The canonical definition is a GIOP semantic synthesis supported by external evidence, not a quotation from an external ontology.

## Validation Notes

The entry intentionally does not assert `Observation ⊂ Measurement` or `Measurement ⊂ Observation` as a canonical hierarchy. Their relationship is operationally close but their primary semantic responsibilities differ.
