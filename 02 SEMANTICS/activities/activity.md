# Activity

**Semantic ID:** `SEM-ACTIVITY-001`  
**Preferred Name:** Activity  
**Semantic Class:** Activity  
**Domain:** Cross-domain semantic foundation  
**Status:** Active  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Primary Responsibility:** A temporal occurrence in which an action, operation, observation, measurement, acquisition, transformation, calibration, or related performance occurs.

## 5W1H Orientation

### What
An Activity is an occurrence that takes place over time. It describes something being performed, carried out, observed, measured, acquired, transformed, calibrated, or otherwise enacted rather than a persistent thing, specification, or resulting information object.

### Why
Activity provides the semantic layer for representing what actually happens. It connects entities, conditions, procedures, agents or systems, time, and resulting information without collapsing those responsibilities into one concept.

### Who
An Activity may be performed, initiated, observed, controlled, or otherwise participated in by an Agent or System, depending on the domain and activity type.

### Where
Activity may occur in physical, laboratory, optical, computational, simulated, virtual-production, measurement, imaging, or other operational contexts.

### When
An Activity has temporal extent. Its execution interval may be distinct from the time of the phenomenon being observed and from the time at which its result becomes available.

### How
An Activity may follow a Procedure, use or consume entities, involve an Agent or System, interact with Conditions or States, and generate one or more Results or other entities.

## Semantic Definition
**Activity** is a temporal occurrence in which an action or operation is performed or otherwise takes place. An Activity may use entities, involve agents or systems, occur under specified conditions, follow a procedure, participate in a broader process, and generate results or other entities.

Activity is an occurrence-oriented semantic concept. It does not by itself assert that the occurrence is a transformation process, a prescribed procedure, a result, or a representation.

## Semantic Responsibility
The primary responsibility of Activity is to represent the fact and structure of an occurrence: what was done or occurred, over what temporal extent, with what participating entities or agents, under what relevant circumstances, and with what outputs.

## Core Distinctions

### Activity vs Entity
An Entity is a persistent or identifiable thing or information object. An Activity is an occurrence. A Camera, Sensor, Result, or Representation may participate in or be generated by an Activity but is not thereby an Activity.

### Activity vs Process
An Activity is an occurrence of doing or happening. A Process denotes a transformation, development, progression, or organized course that may extend through time and may contain or organize Activities. `Activity ≠ Process`.

### Activity vs Procedure
A Procedure specifies how an activity should be performed. An Activity is the actual occurrence. `Procedure ≠ Activity`.

### Activity vs Result
A Result is an output or information obtained from an Activity. An Activity produces or generates a Result; it is not the Result. `Activity ≠ Result`.

### Activity vs Representation
A Representation is an information-bearing form. An Activity may generate or transform a Representation, but the occurrence itself is not the representation.

### Activity vs State
A State describes a condition or mode of an entity or system. An Activity describes what occurs over time. An Activity may change or occur under a State, but the two semantic responsibilities remain distinct.

## Activity Patterns
```text
Activity
├── Execution-oriented Activity
│   ├── Observation
│   └── Measurement
├── Acquisition
├── Processing
└── Calibration
```
Execution is a procedure-driven execution pattern and is not a universal synonym or mandatory parent for every Activity.

## Relation to Procedure
A Procedure is a reusable or prescribed description of how an activity is to be performed. The semantic dependency is `Procedure → specifies → Activity / Execution`; `specifies` remains a conceptual dependency unless separately admitted to the canonical relation vocabulary.

## Relation to Process
A Process may organize, contain, or describe a temporally extended course involving multiple Activity occurrences. Activity remains the occurrence-level concept.

## Temporal Semantics
Activity is inherently temporal. Relevant temporal information may include start time, end time, duration where derivable, phenomenon time when applicable, and result time. These must not be silently collapsed when they represent different events.

## Actors and Systems
An Activity may be associated with a human Agent, organizational Agent, instrument or System, software or computational infrastructure, or multiple participating entities. Participation does not imply that a participating object is itself an Activity.

## Inputs and Used Entities
An Activity may use entities such as Scene, Object, Material, Light Source, Camera, Lens, Sensor, Measuring System, Procedure, Algorithm, Software, prior Results, or Representations. Exact inputs are activity-specific.

## Outputs and Generated Entities
An Activity may generate Result, Measurement Result, acquired data, Representation, transformed information, calibration information, or other domain entities. Output type is not synonymous with Activity type.

## Cross-Domain Significance
Activity is the operational bridge between static semantic concepts and actual occurrence records. It supports imaging, measurement, observation, acquisition, computational processing, calibration, experimentation, simulation, and other domains in which provenance and temporal execution matter.

## Trust / Provenance
Activity records are provenance-sensitive. Where an occurrence is asserted as factual, the record should preserve source, evidence, validation state, authority, confidence or review state, temporal information, and unresolved questions as applicable.

## Validation
Cross-layer validation confirms the Activity boundary against Entity, Procedure, Process, Result, Representation, State, and the specialized Activity concepts in this batch. The Activity identity is approved for active canonical use within the scoped batch.
